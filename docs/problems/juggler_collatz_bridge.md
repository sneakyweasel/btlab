# Juggler: what exactly is its relation to Collatz?

## Problem

Paper B's survivor count turned out to be a known Collatz sequence
(`J-paper-b-survivors-are-oeis-a076227`). Is that a coincidence of small
integers, a structural identity, or an equivalence of the two problems?

## Exact statement

Two questions, both decidable here.

1. Do the two maps induce the same additive walk? Accelerated Collatz sends `x`
   to `x/2` or `(3x+1)/2`, so `log x` moves by `-log 2` or `+log(3/2)`. Juggler
   sends `n` to `floor(sqrt n)` or `floor(n^(3/2))`, so `log n` is *multiplied*
   by `1/2` or `3/2`, hence `log log n` moves by `-log 2` or `+log(3/2)`.
2. Is `N_d`, the count of length-`d` words with `3^o >= 2^m` at every prefix,
   equal to the number of residue classes modulo `2^d` on which the Collatz
   stopping time is not constant -- when checked by running Collatz rather than
   by quoting a database?

## Current literature

The Collatz half is classical. That `x mod 2^d` determines the first `d`
parities, bijectively onto `{0,1}^d`, is Terras 1976, carried here as
`terras-1976-stopping-time` and already labelled `KNOWN`. The sequence is
OEIS A076227, in the database since 2002. Nothing in this dossier is a new
Collatz theorem; what is new here is the laboratory's own understanding of
which of its results were about Collatz all along.

## Branch budget

- **Target:** the precise relation between the two problems -- identity,
  equivalence, or neither.
- **Novelty hypothesis:** none mathematically. The value is a partition of the
  laboratory's own results into shared and Juggler-specific.
- **Falsifier:** the walks having different step sets, or the counts differing
  at any depth where both can be computed.
- **Already killed by?:** none. `negative_knowledge` kills the balanced-ternary
  warp `W` as a conjugacy and reversal as a Collatz intertwiner; neither is this
  question, which is about the multiplier semigroup and not about `W`.
- **Existing machinery:** the height program; the log-log clock of
  `juggler_lachesis_loglog_clock`, which already reduces the exponent walk mod 1
  to the rotation by `log2(3/2)`.
- **Maximum Phase-0 scope:** one probe, its test, this dossier, ledger rows, a
  journal entry.
- **Promotion criterion:** both identifications verified by computation here.
- **Stop criterion:** if the step sets differ, record a near-miss and `CLOSE`.

Not used, and deliberately so. The `W` warp as a Collatz intertwiner is already

## Candidate operations / invariants

The multiplier semigroup generated by `3/2` and `1/2`; the additive walk
`o log 3 - m log 2`; the non-contraction condition `3^o >= 2^m`; the barrier
word; Terras's parity-vector bijection.

## Experiments

`python -m research.juggler_sequence.collatz_bridge`.

**The walk.** From the seed `10^20 + 1` the measured increments of `log log n`
are `+0.405465108108` and `-0.693147180560` against targets `log(3/2)` and
`-log 2`, agreeing to `2e-16` on the first two steps. The floor costs `O(1)` in
`n` and so `O(1/n)` in `log n`; at `n = 7` the same increments are wrong by a
percent, which is the size condition made visible rather than hidden.

**The count.** For `d = 4 .. 10` the parity map is bijective on `Z/2^d`, and the
number of residue classes whose Collatz stopping time is not constant is
`3, 4, 8, 13, 19, 38, 64` -- exactly `N_d`. Computed by running Collatz on eight
representatives per class, not by quoting OEIS.

## Conjectures

None.

## Counterexamples

None. The near-miss worth recording is the one this branch does **not** claim:
the maps are not conjugate, and no conjugacy is proposed. They realise the same
additive walk on different variables, which is weaker and is all the evidence
supports.

## Formalization

None here. The shared combinatorics is already Lean-verified on the Juggler
side -- `PaperBCertificateRecursion`, `PaperBCertificateLengths`,
`PaperBJumpTransposition` -- and by this identification those theorems are
statements about the Collatz parity semigroup as well. Nothing about the
relation itself needs formalising: it is two definitions and one line of
arithmetic.

## Results

`J-juggler-is-collatz-one-exponential-up` -- `COMPUTATIONALLY VERIFIED`. Both
maps realise the additive walk with steps `log(3/2)` and `-log 2`; Collatz on
`log x`, Juggler on `log log n`. Hence the non-contracting words are the same
words and `N_d` is the same integer, verified against Collatz directly.

`J-word-density-results-are-not-juggler-specific` -- `REPARAMETERIZATION`. Any
laboratory result that depends only on word densities is a statement about the
shared semigroup and is true of Collatz too.

**Where the two part, and it is the whole of Paper B.** For Collatz the parity
word is a function of `x mod 2^d` and every word has density exactly `2^(-d)`:
arithmetic, exact, free. For Juggler the word is a function of `frac(n^(3/2))`
and its relatives, and equal densities are Hypothesis FD -- open, and the
analytic burden of the entire paper. Same combinatorics, incompatible
arithmetic.

**Three consequences that cut both ways.**

- The asymptotic of `J-paper-b-jump-spectrum-is-the-survivor-sequence` is
  **unconditional for Collatz** and conditional for Juggler, because Terras
  supplies there what FD only conjectures here. That raises the stakes on the
  priority question, which is open.
- Collatz is a **free validator** for any FD-conditional derivation depending
  only on word densities: there, such a statement is a theorem, so a failure
  would indict the derivation rather than FD. The laboratory does not currently
  use it this way.
- Any result depending only on word densities is **not Juggler-specific**,
  however Juggler-shaped its wording. By that test the survivor recursion, the
  empty-window theorem, the transposition cost, the jump spectrum, `a_1` and the
  ladder profile all belong to both problems.

## Decision

`PROMOTE` -- both identifications verified by computation, and the partition
they force is worth carrying. The methodological half is recorded in
[negative_knowledge.md](../negative_knowledge.md).

## Publication assessment

Status: `EXPLORATORY`, and probably not publishable at all. It moves no bound in
either problem, proves nothing new about Collatz beyond restating Terras, and
proves nothing about Juggler. Juggler is **not** an easier Collatz: it is
Collatz's word problem plus a Weyl-sum problem, and both stall at the same wall.
Its value is as a separator -- it preserves the combinatorics exactly while
removing the 2-adic rigidity, so it shows which parts of the Collatz problem
need the 2-adics and which need only the words. On that reading Paper B's real
contribution is machinery for proving analytically what Terras gets
arithmetically, and that is the part which could not have come from Collatz.

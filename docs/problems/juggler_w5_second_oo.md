## Problem

After odd \(x_5\) produces odd \(y<n^4\), what inherited
corridor does the next `OO` occupy, and is the first
integer threshold \(n^5\)?

## Exact statement

Assume \(n\ge 2\) follows \(W_5=M(\mathtt{OOE})^5\) with
\(M=\mathtt{OOEOOOEOOEEOOE}\), that \(x_5=T_{W_5}(n)\) is
odd, and that \(y=T(x_5)\) is odd. Write \(z=T(y)\) and
\(u=T(z)\). The Phase-0 questions are:

1. the inherited envelopes of \(z\) and of \(u\) when \(z\)
   is odd, not generic \(3/2\) growth;
2. the first integer \(m\) with \(z<n^m\) and with
   \(u<n^m\);
3. whether \(u<n^5\) survives;
4. the even-\(z\) and even-\(u\) pullbacks;
5. whether two further odd steps raise the integer ceiling
   by exactly one.

Lower bounds \(z\ge n^4\) and \(u\ge n^4\) are **not**
axioms of the lost fourth-power cell.

## Counterexamples

\(n^5\) cell, or that two further odds raise the integer
ceiling by one, is **REFUTED** by

\[
3^{22}>5\cdot 2^{32},\qquad 3^{22}<8\cdot 2^{32}.

\(n^{5/2}\).

the Phase-0 window \(12\le n<801\).

## Results

Classification **W5_SECOND_OO_GREEN**.

If \(y\) is odd, then

\[
z^{2147483648}\le n^{10460353203}<n^{10737418240}=(n^5)^{2147483648},
\]

so

\[
z<n^{3^{21}/2^{31}}<n^5.
\]

This is the first \(n^5\) corridor. Crossing \(n^4\) is
possible, not forced.

If \(z\) is also odd, then

\[
u<n^{3^{22}/2^{32}}<n^8.
\]

The \(n^5\) candidate does not survive the second odd
letter of the `OO`. The first integer is \(n^8\).

Even \(z\) returns to a previously controlled band:

\[
z\text{ even}\Rightarrow T(z)<n^{5/2}<n^3.
\]

Even \(u\) returns below \(n^4\). Neither even landing
restarts \(L\). Scale-band return is not episode
recurrence: the itinerary-progress is \(W_5+\mathtt{OO}\) or
\(W_5+\mathtt{OOO}\), not \(L\).

This is not \(Z_5\), not a length-11 census, not a four-even
assembler, and not a halt theorem.

## Decision

**PROMOTE**. The first new corridor after odd \(y\) starts
`OO` is fifth-power for the first extra `O`. Completing
that `OO` by a second odd letter is **not** fifth-power:
it is an eighth-power cell. Even landings reset to known
bands (\(n^{5/2}\) or \(n^4\)). The one-rung-per-two-odds
pattern fails here.

Best next question: if \(u\) is odd, does the next `O`
stay below a tighter inherited ceiling than \(n^{11}\), or
is that eleventh-power landing the leftover?

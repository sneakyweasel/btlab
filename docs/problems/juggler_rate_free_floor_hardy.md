**2. Minimal missing theorem (UNBUILT_DOOR).** Full Haar
equidistribution is stronger than termination needs. After an
odd landing \(x\), the next letter is E if and only if
\(\lfloor x^{3/2}\rfloor\) is even, if and only if
\(\{x^{3/2}/2\}\in[0,1/2)\). Lemma B of
`J-rate-free-density-one` therefore asks only for a node-wise
liminf of that half-interval mass strictly above
\(\beta_*=1-\log 2/\log 3\).

The half-interval indicator is Riemann integrable. For every
\(\beta\in(\beta_*,1/2]\) there is a continuous tent
\(0\le\psi\le 1_{[0,1/2)}\) with \(\int\psi>\beta\) (the tent
that rises on \([0,\varepsilon]\), stays \(1\) on
\([\varepsilon,1/2-\varepsilon]\), and falls on
\([1/2-\varepsilon,1/2]\), has integral \(\tfrac12-\varepsilon\);
\(\varepsilon=0.12\) gives \(0.38>\beta_*\)). A trigonometric
polynomial of some finite degree \(K=K(\beta)\) approximates
\(\psi\) uniformly, so Weyl's criterion on **finitely many
fixed harmonics** of the single phase \(\{x^{3/2}\}\) implies
the \(\beta\)-fallback.

This is a complexity reduction, not a species reduction. At the
first unproved node the phase is a coordinate of the floor-Hardy
tower. Each harmonic \(e(k v^{9/4})\) with \(k\) fixed and
nonzero still has derivative \(\asymp n^{7/8}\gg 1\)
(`J-horizontal-axis-species`); finite \(K\) does not put the
sums into Theorem R, Boshernitzan, Richter, or Frantzikinakis.

The weakest unbuilt statement that would finish the route is
therefore: for each fixed surviving node, the Weyl averages of
\(\{x^{3/2}\}\) through degree \(K(\beta)\) vanish. A convenient
stronger form, still unbuilt, is equidistribution of
\(n\mapsto\{\lfloor n^{3/2}\rfloor^{9/4}\}\). The missing
published lemma that would give the stronger form is Leibman /
Richter for the composition \(a^{f(\lfloor h(n)\rfloor)}\) with
\(f\) a *nonlinear* Hardy monomial (the circle case:
\(\{f(\lfloor h(n)\rfloor)\}\) equidistributes whenever
\(\{f(h(n))\}\) does and \(f'\circ h\not\to 0\)).

**3. Direct floor treatment recreates \(n^{15/8}\theta\)
(KNOWN; corollary of `J-horizontal-axis-species`).** Mean
value: for \(\alpha>0\), \(X=n^{3/2}\), \(v=\lfloor X\rfloor\),
\(\theta=\{X\}\),
\[
\bigl\lvert v^\alpha-X^\alpha\bigr\rvert
=\alpha\,\xi^{\alpha-1}\theta
=O\bigl(X^{\alpha-1}\bigr),
\qquad\xi\in(v,X).
\]
The defect is \(o(1)\) if and only if \(\alpha<1\). For
\(\alpha=9/4\) it is \(\tfrac94 n^{15/8}\theta+O(n^{3/8})\),
already HH, forbidden as a proof route by
`J-nested-floor-without-W-family`. Unwinding through \(\theta\)
produces no new information.

Keeping \(v\) as an integer is the intrinsic reading: the
sequence is \(\{m^{9/4}\}\) along the Piatetski–Shapiro image
\(m=v(n)\). Boshernitzan applies to \(\{m^{9/4}\}\) as
\(m\) runs through \(\mathbb N\). The image has gaps
\(\asymp m^{1/3}\) and density zero. Rate-free unique
ergodicity of the full-integer sequence does not pass to a
zero-density subsequence. The published exception is the
*linear* path \(b^{\lfloor a(n)\rfloor}\) (Frantzikinakis).
A nonlinear Hardy path sampled at PS times is exactly
\(a^{f(\lfloor h(n)\rfloor)}\).

**4. No characteristic-factor-free unique-ergodicity principle
applies (KNOWN).** Weyl's criterion on the horizontal torus is
characteristic-factor-free and is the laboratory reduction
already recorded. It asks for the same Weyl sums. Unique
ergodicity of a *transformation* does not apply: the map
\(n\mapsto\bigl(\tfrac32 v^{3/4},v^{3/2},\tfrac12 v^{9/4}\bigr)\)
is a non-autonomous path, not an orbit of a fixed nilrotation.
Unique ergodicity of a *Hardy sequence* is Boshernitzan /
Richter and requires Hardy-in-\(n\) entries. There is no third
published principle. Reproducing \(A\{\Delta B\}\) is the
already-closed PET route and is not taken.

**5. Horizontal versus vertical.** The vertical is
Riemann-integrable (`J-heisenberg-vertical-riemann`); Haar
identification of the orbit would pass to it. The unresolved
issue is equidistribution of the horizontal triple, i.e. the
abelian axis \(\{v^{9/4}\}\). That is the door of (1)–(4).

DESCRIPTION (paste as HTML)

<p>The Juggler map is the nonlinear integer map J(n) = floor(sqrt(n)) if n is even, and J(n) = floor(n^{3/2}) if n is odd. It is conjectured that every positive integer eventually reaches 1. This paper does not prove that conjecture. It proves period lower bounds for a hypothetical nontrivial cycle, once a verified descent floor is given.</p>
<p>A realized parity word obeys a power envelope, so a cycle word is formally expanding. Floor remainders must finance the surplus 3^o &minus; 2^L. At a cycle minimum this yields the financing inequality n log n &middot; (3^o &minus; 2^L) &le; L &middot; 3^o. This inequality is the main theorem; every numerical period bound is an instantiation of it, or of its walk-charge refinement, at a certified descent floor. Combined with the independently verified descent that every start through 10^6 reaches 1, any nontrivial cycle has period at least 25781. A walk-charge envelope &mdash; transport of the floor losses to a reduced base, identification of the extremal exponent walk as a rotation itinerary, and a Denjoy&ndash;Koksma bound over certified Ostrowski blocks, census-free on the window [50508, 301994) &mdash; extends the exclusion at a certified laboratory floor N0 = 26254995 to period at least 176251. The same certified kill criterion at a second certified floor, N0 = 162849448, gives period at least 478245. The main numerical result evaluates it once more at the third certified floor N0 = 350000000: any nontrivial Juggler cycle has period at least 780239. A separate inverse-cell analysis shows that every nontrivial cycle has at least four even letters and hence period at least eleven, with no descent floor. A floor-free gap transfer, n log n &middot; min(o log 3 &minus; L log 2, 1) &le; 2L, combined with Rhin's effective irrationality measure, excludes every cycle with L^{14.3} &le; n log n / 915 and reduces the no-cycle problem to the long regime, where it remains open. The core lemmas are mechanized in Lean 4; the descent floors and the per-length kill tables are independently certified computations, and the paper does not claim to be formally verified as a whole. Two companion manuscripts use the power envelope and the certified floor as inputs (parity equidistribution of nested floor powers; fate contagion and the almost-all reduction of termination); Section 6.1 records what they add to the cycle problem and what they do not.</p>
